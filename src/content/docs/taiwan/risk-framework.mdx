MODA's framework is the shared vocabulary; sector regulators turn it into binding rules. Finance is furthest ahead.

## MODA AI Risk Classification Framework

Version 1.0 of the 人工智慧風險分類框架 was issued on 7 July 2026 under Article 16 of the Basic Act.

- **Structure:** three risk groups (technical design defects; operation after deployment and human–machine interaction; societal, structural and environmental impact) with 20 sub-types.
- **Method:** four steps: inventory the use cases, identify risks, assess them, respond.
- **High risk** means serious harm to national security, fundamental rights, life, property, social order or the environment.
- **Binding force:** none on private-sector AI directly. It is a reference for regulators, and it excludes military AI.
- **Rollout:** the Ministry of Labor, the FSC, the Ministry of Health and Welfare and the Ministry of Education apply it first, followed by transport, economic affairs and the NCC. Regulators' responses can scale from self-regulatory guidance, to transparency duties, to prior review or third-party verification, to prohibition.

## Financial Supervisory Commission

- **Guidelines for the Use of AI in the Financial Industry** (金融業運用人工智慧(AI)指引), in effect since 20 June 2024. They are administrative guidance, not a mandatory rule, built on six principles that include system robustness and security. A 2026 revision covering agentic AI and risk classification was under study in July 2026 ([CNA](https://www.cna.com.tw/news/afe/202406200383.aspx), secondary).
- **14 July 2026 reminder on frontier-model attack risk.** The FSC asked institutions to act on seven fronts: zero-trust architecture; continuous monitoring; security built into CI/CD; third-party governance and SBOM management; joint defence and F-ISAC intelligence sharing; readiness and resilience drills; and defensive AI with automated governance.
- **2026 supervision priorities** include SBOM-based supply-chain controls, zero trust in high-risk scenarios, and a planned AI system security and testing guide drawing on the OWASP GenAI Security Project and MITRE ATLAS ([iThome](https://www.ithome.com.tw/news/173594), secondary).

## AI Product and System Evaluation Center (AIEC)

MODA's Administration for Digital Industries set up the centre with the National Institute of Cyber Security and ITRI, under directions issued on 18 March 2024. It supervises AI verification bodies and testing laboratories and evaluates products on ten items, with security (資安) as its own item alongside safety, explainability, resiliency, fairness, accuracy, transparency, accountability, reliability and privacy ([AIEC](https://www.aiec.org.tw/web/guest/background)). It is an administrative scheme, not a statutory certification.
