One hundred papers and technical reports, chosen for what a team testing or deploying LLM applications can reuse: a dataset, a scoring method, a defence to compare against, or a result that changes how a test should be designed. Dates are the month a paper first appeared. Venue names say when a paper is a preprint or has a single author. Each paper links to the items of the [LLM security evaluation matrix](/red-team/evaluation-matrix/) it informs.

Four results recur across the list. Simple tool firewalls score perfectly on several public agent-injection benchmarks, so a passing score on a static set says little about an adaptive attacker. Automated judges are often the weakest part of a red-team result: in one study, adding harmless framing around a response changed LLM judges' verdicts in 57 to 100% of cases. In one multi-agent benchmark, messages between agents leaked private data more than twice as often as final answers did. And in one tool-poisoning benchmark the more capable models were often the more susceptible.
